# Quick read rubrics

Two scores, shown in this order: **Company fit**, then **Role fit**. Each factor is scored 1–10, then weighted to a score out of 100. Round to a whole number.

## Scoring rules
- **5 means typical.** Most jobs and most companies are a 5 on most factors. That's not a bad score; it's an honest one.
- **Not verified caps at 6.** If a factor rests on an Inferred claim or a guess, it can't score above 6.
- **9–10 needs a verified reason,** named in the why line.
- **If more than 2 factors in one score are 9 or higher, re-check them.** That's usually generous scoring.
- **A skipped Intake answer caps the factor that depends on it at 6.**

## Thin evidence
When one search finds little, most factors sit at 5 and Company fit lands near 50. That means "not enough known," not "bad company." Private companies hit this most often. So:
- A fact from the company's own newsroom or press release is **Verified** and can lift a factor above 6.
- If 3 or more Company fit factors are 5 because nothing was found, add this to the why line: "Mostly unknowns; Go deeper would sharpen this."

## Company fit = (Stability × 3.5) + (Momentum × 2.5) + (Leadership × 2) + (Pull × 2)

**Stability**
- 9–10: Verified: public and profitable, or recently well funded (a round the company itself announced counts), with no layoffs in the last 12 months.
- 6–8: Funded and growing, with some mixed news.
- 5: Typical, or not enough found to say.
- 1–4: Layoffs in the last 12 months (especially in this team's area) or money trouble.

**Momentum**
- 9–10: Verified recent growth: new funding, major launches, big customer wins.
- 6–8: Some positive news.
- 5: Quiet, or nothing found.
- 1–4: Shrinking, a pivot away from its core, or losing ground to competitors.

**Leadership**
- 9–10: Verified stable senior team, with no major exits in 18 months.
- 6–8: One or two changes that look normal.
- 5: Nothing found either way.
- 1–4: Heavy turnover, a new CEO followed by departures, or leaders of this team's area recently gone.

**Pull**
- 9: The company is on their dream companies list.
- 7: The user said in this chat that they're excited about it.
- 5: Everything else.

## Role fit = (Role match × 4) + (Work you want × 3) + (Level and pay × 3)

**Role match:** the posting's must-haves compared with their most recent role, years, top skills, and target roles.
- 9–10: The title is one of their target roles, and their answers show every must-have.
- 6–8: Meets most must-haves; one gap that's close to what they've done.
- 5: Meets about half, or the posting is too vague to tell.
- 1–4: Misses a hard must-have (a license, years in a specific field, managing people they haven't managed), or the title is far from their targets.

**Work you want:** the day-to-day duties compared with the work that makes them lose track of time, and their target roles.
- 9–10: The core duties are the work they named.
- 6–8: Some of the duties are that work.
- 5: Neutral, or the posting doesn't describe the day-to-day.
- 1–4: The day-to-day is mostly work they didn't name, or the opposite of it.

**Level and pay:** the level compared with their experience; posted pay compared with their pay floor.
- 9–10: Posted pay is clearly above the floor, and the level matches their experience.
- 6–8: Pay is at or near the floor, or the level is one notch off.
- 5: Pay not posted and the level fits. Don't guess the pay.
- 1–4: Clearly too junior, or a big stretch in level.

## Suggested call

| Role fit | Company fit | Suggested call |
|---|---|---|
| 70 or higher | 55 or higher | Pursue |
| 55–69, or 70+ with Company fit 40–54 | | Maybe |
| Below 55, or Company fit below 40 | | Pass |

Overrides:
- Any gate ✗ → **Pass**.
- A gate ? on location or a deal-breaker → **Maybe** at most.
- Company fit only (no specific job) → **Maybe** at most.

These are starting bands, set before real-world testing. If the weights change, re-test the bands on a handful of real jobs. Weights and bands work as one system.
