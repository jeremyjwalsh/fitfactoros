# Stage checklists

One short checklist for each step after you decide to go after a job. They're habits that helped in real searches, not rules. Skip anything that doesn't fit.

They line up with the rungs already on your ladder, so your Sheet doesn't change:

| Your job's Ladder stage | Checklist |
|---|---|
| Researched, and you chose Pursue | [Applied](#applied): before you hit submit |
| Applied | [Applied](#applied), then [Waiting](#waiting) |
| Heard back | [Interviewing](#interviewing) if it's a yes, [Closing the loop](#closing-the-loop) if it's a no |
| Interviewing | [Interviewing](#interviewing), then [Waiting](#waiting) between rounds |
| Offer | [Offer](#offer), then [Negotiate](#negotiate), then [Accept or decline](#accept-or-decline) |

## Applied

Before you hit submit:

1. **Tailor your resume for this job.** Say **tailor my resume** in the chat where you fit factored it. Read every line of the result, then save it as a PDF.
2. **Warm path? Reach out first.** Write to your contact, then apply 2 to 3 days later, so they can flag you before your application lands in the pile.
3. **Save the posting text.** Postings come down, and you'll want the exact wording before your first interview.
4. **Write down what the posting promises:** pay range, level, location, and start date, if it gives them. You'll compare against these later.
5. **Log it the same day.** Say **log this: applied to [Company]**. It moves the job to Applied and adds it to your Activity log.

Then set the job's Next step to "Follow up if no reply," about a week out.

## Interviewing

**Before each round**
- Find out who you'll meet and what each person does. **Plan one question for each person**, so the call becomes a conversation.
- **Prepare stories, not sentences.** Three or four real examples, with numbers, that you can tell in under two minutes. Too much script makes you stiff; your best moments are often the unscripted ones.
- **Prepare for the hardest question you can think of.** It often comes up nearly word for word.
- Presenting? **Do a timed dry run out loud**, and cut until it fits.
- **Re-check every fact you'll say about the company**: is it confirmed, an estimate, or your own guess? A wrong number, or the wrong company name, costs more than leaving it out.

**During**
- **Short answers first.** Let them ask for more.
- **Say "I" for what you did.** Credit your team, but don't give away your own credit.
- **Aim questions at a person**, by name, not at the room.
- **When someone corrects you, take it and build on it.** Don't defend.

**After each round, the same day**
- Write down what they asked, what they seemed to care about most, and anything said about pay, level, timeline, or next steps, in their words.
- Ask yourself: **did this make you want the job more or less? Why?**
- Send thank-you notes within a day. You write and send them.
- Say **log this: interview with [Company]** and paste your notes. You get an Activity row and an Interviews row.

**Optional: know how you come across.** A personality self-check like DiSC can help you notice your habits under pressure (for example, talking long or rushing to a fix). It's for your own reflection only: FitFactorOS never asks for it, never saves it, and never uses it in a fit score. The official version, Everything DiSC, is published by Wiley and is paid. Many free "DISC" tests use the same idea but aren't Wiley's. They can still be useful; just know what you're taking. Some of these sites also offer to guess someone else's personality from their profile. Don't use that on the people interviewing you.

### A meeting note-taker (optional)

A meeting note-taker records a call and writes up notes. In a job search it helps with three things:
- **Debriefs after each round:** what they asked and what they seemed to care about.
- **Catching what a recruiter says out loud:** pay numbers, level, start date, and promises. These matter later, when you [negotiate](#negotiate).
- **Prep for the next round:** what came up, and what to follow up on.

**Ask first, every time.** Tell everyone at the start of the call that you'd like to record it, and get their OK before you start. Some states require everyone on the call to agree. In Massachusetts, secretly recording a conversation without the prior permission of everyone in it is a crime ([Massachusetts General Laws, chapter 272, section 99](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIV/TitleI/Chapter272/Section99)). Rules differ by state and country, and people on a call can be in different places, so the simplest habit is to ask everyone, every time. If anyone says no, don't record; take notes by hand. This isn't legal advice.

**Which one?** Any note-taker you trust works, and many video-call tools have their own. Granola is one option. As of Nov 6, 2026, it has a free Basic plan that shows your notes from the last 30 days, and paid plans start at $14 per user per month ([Granola: subscriptions and billing](https://docs.granola.ai/article/subscriptions-and-billing)). Plans change, so check before you count on it. FitFactorOS has no connection to Granola or any other note-taker.

**Using your notes with FitFactorOS.** Paste your notes after **log this: interview with [Company]**. Claude fills in the Interviews row only from what's in your notes and what you tell it, never from guesses. Granola also has a connector in Claude's connector directory, built by Granola; on Granola's free plan it reaches only your last 30 days of notes, without transcripts ([Granola's Claude page](https://granola.ai/integrations/claude)). FitFactorOS doesn't use it yet, and pasting works just as well. Your notes hold other people's words, so keep them private.

## Waiting

- **Silence isn't an answer yet.** A yes often takes longer than a no: more people have to sign off.
- **Before you read into a delay,** check the date they actually gave you, holidays, and time zones. It's easy to guess someone's time zone wrong from an email timestamp.
- **Look at how quickly this person usually replies to you.** Once it's well past that, a short check-in is fair.
- **Follow up once, briefly,** after the date they gave you has passed. Add something useful if you can, such as a short answer to something they asked.
- **Keep other jobs moving.** Waiting on one isn't a reason to stop.

## Offer

**On the offer call: collect, don't negotiate.**
- Say thank you, write everything down, and accept nothing on the call. "Okay, got it" is a complete answer.
- Ask for it in writing, and when they need your answer. Asking for a day or two is normal.
- Ask six things: the level and its pay band; base pay, bonus or commission, and how it ramps up; equity (how much, how it vests, and whether and when you could sell it); any sign-on bonus; the start date; and the path to the next level.
- Ask whether your start date changes your bonus, your vesting, or when benefits begin.

**Then sort the offer into four kinds of pay, plus benefits:**
- **Guaranteed:** base pay and any sign-on bonus.
- **Target:** bonus or commission if you hit 100% of the goal.
- **Conditional:** anything that depends on rules: quotas, payment schedules, floors, caps, or pay you'd have to give back if you leave early.
- **Speculative:** equity. It isn't cash until you can sell it, and you may not be able to for years.
- **Benefits:** put a dollar figure on health premiums, retirement match, and time off, compared with what you have now.

**Read the plan documents.** Where they differ from what you were told, ask about the difference. The written version is usually what counts.

**Check it against your Intake answers:** your pay floor and your deal-breakers.

Free resource: [CareerOneStop Salary Finder](https://www.careeronestop.org/Salary), from the U.S. Department of Labor, shows typical pay by occupation and location.

## Negotiate

- **Line up what was said with what's offered:** "Earlier: X. Now: Y." Your notes from recruiter calls are your best evidence.
- **Every ask needs a fact behind it:** a number they said, a posted range, published pay data, or a real other offer. No made-up numbers, and no hinting at an offer you don't have.
- **Know your real best option if this falls through.** Negotiators call it your BATNA. It might be another offer, your current job, or staying in the search. Knowing it lets you ask calmly.
- **Know the range where you'd both say yes.** Negotiators call it the ZOPA: from the least you'd take to the most they can likely pay. Make your ask inside it.
- **Ask "How much room is there?"**, not "Is there any room?"
- **Don't point to a past salary from a different kind of role.** It invites "that was a different job."
- **Make it "help me get to yes,"** and be flexible on the mix: base, sign-on, equity, or start date.
- **Send your ask before you confirm a start date.** Agreeing to a date can set the paperwork in motion.
- **Gut-check with a person you trust before you send.** Drafting with AI can make a plan feel more certain than it is.
- **Know when to stop.** Once they've moved and what's left looks fixed, take the win.

Free resource: [Negotiate a Job Offer like a Pro](https://cloudfront.careeronestop.org/Videos/JobSearchVideos/negotiate-a-job-offer-like-a-pro-video.aspx), a short video from CareerOneStop.

## Accept or decline

- **Get the final offer in writing** and check it against your notes before you sign.
- **Accepting after negotiating?** Close the loop warmly. Thank the people you negotiated with; you'll be working with them.
- **Declining?** Say no clearly and kindly, and leave the door open.
- **Tell the people who helped you:** your contacts, referrals, and anyone who gave you advice.
- **Withdraw from your other searches politely** once you've signed.
- **Update your Sheet:** set the job's Status to **Closed – offer** (or **Withdrawn** for any you leave).
- **Collecting unemployment?** Report the new job the way your state requires. Your state's unemployment agency has the rules.

## Closing the loop

When the answer is no:
- **It still counts.** A no is a closed loop, and your ladder already shows it as Heard back. Set the job's Status to **Closed – no**.
- **Reply with a short thank-you.** If it feels right, ask whether there's anything they'd share, or another role there worth a look.
- **Write down what you learned** in the job's Notes or Interviews row, while it's fresh.
- **Thank your contact** if someone helped you get in.
